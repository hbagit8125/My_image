Every few weeks someone asks me whether AI is a bubble. They usually expect a
yes or a no. I build with language models for a living, and my honest answer is
that the question mixes up two different things.

## Two questions hiding in one

The first question is about money: are companies and investors paying more for
AI than it will earn back soon? The second is about the technology: does it do
useful work?

A bubble is an answer to the first question, not the second. Prices can run far
ahead of value even when the thing underneath is real. The dot-com crash wiped
out a lot of companies, and the internet still went on to change everything.
Railways, electricity and telecom all had their own versions of that story.

So I can believe that expectations are inflated and still spend my days building
with these models without feeling like a fraud.

## What looks like a bubble to me

Some patterns I see make me cautious:

- **Demos that never become products.** A model that works on ten hand-picked
  examples is a long way from one that works on ten thousand real ones.
- **"AI" added to things that did not need it.** If a search box or a simple
  rule would do the job, a language model mostly adds cost and unpredictability.
- **Promises with no way to measure them.** If nobody can say how accuracy is
  checked, the claim is marketing.

None of this means the technology is fake. It means a lot of what is being sold
has not been tested against real use yet.

## What does not look like a bubble

The work that convinces me is boring, and that is the point. It has a narrow
task, a clear measure of success and a person who checks the output.

In my own experience, fine-tuning a model for one well-defined job beats asking a
general model to do everything. The gains are not magic. They come from clean
data, careful evaluation and many small fixes. When a new version is better, I
can show it with numbers, and the people who use it notice.

That kind of value does not depend on hype. It would still be there if every
headline about AI disappeared tomorrow.

## How I try to stay honest

A few habits help me separate what is real from what is noise:

1. **Start with the problem, not the model.** If I cannot describe the task
   without mentioning AI, I do not understand it yet.
2. **Measure before and after.** A change that cannot be measured cannot be
   called an improvement.
3. **Keep a person in the loop** wherever a mistake would be costly.
4. **Count the full cost.** Serving, monitoring and fixing a model in production
   is most of the work, and it rarely appears in the demo.

## So, is it a bubble?

Probably, in parts. Some valuations and promises will not survive contact with
reality, and some of the excitement will fade.

But a correction in prices would not undo what works. The useful systems will
still be useful, and the people who know how to build them carefully will still
be needed. I would rather be one of those people than spend my time guessing
when the music stops.
